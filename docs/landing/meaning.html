What the word means

The lipstick tree

Achiote comes from the Nahuatl āchiotl, the word the Mexica used for the seeds of Bixa orellana, a shrub native to the tropical Americas. The plant bears prickly pods, and inside each pod, waxy seeds are wrapped in a bright red-orange paste. That paste, the aril, is the spice, the dye, and the colour. It has been painted on skin, woven into cloth, and stirred into cooking pots for thousands of years.

Because its pigment coats so vividly, the tree earned the nickname lipstick tree. Today the same compound, bixin, extracted from the aril, colours butter, cheese, and processed foods worldwide under the additive code E160b. The industrial name is annatto, drawn from the Carib and Galibi languages of the Caribbean. But in village markets from Oaxaca to Manila, the seed still goes by older names.

Botanical

Bixa orellana

A shrub in the Bixaceae family, native to the Neotropics. Named for the Amazonian explorer Francisco de Orellana.

The spice

The red aril

The waxy coating around each seed, not the seed itself, carries the pigment. It stains everything it touches: fingers, cloth, oil, masa.

Global trade

E160b (annatto)

One of the oldest natural food colourants still in use. Found in cheddar, butter, margarine, and smoked fish across continents.

Around the world

The same seed, many tongues

Every region that grows or trades the seed has given it a name. Often the name comes from the local Indigenous language (Nahuatl, Tupi, Taíno, or Carib), and then passes into Spanish, Portuguese, English, or Tagalog. Below is a reference of the best-established names, each with its region and origin.

Mexico · Central America · Latin America

Achiote

The most widespread name in the Spanish-speaking Americas. The default term in Mexican, Guatemalan, and Central American cooking for both the seeds and the recado paste.

← Nahuatl āchiotl

English · global food trade

Annatto

The international trade name. Used on ingredient labels, in food regulation (additive E160b), and in English-language culinary writing.

← Carib / Galibi

Brazil

Urucum

The common Portuguese name in Brazil. The colouring paste (colorau) is a staple in every Brazilian kitchen, tinting rice, stews, and farofa a deep gold.

← Tupi uruku, "red"

Cuba · Dominican Republic · Puerto Rico

Bija / Bijol

In the Spanish Caribbean, bija refers to the seeds; bijol is the commercial seasoning paste or powder. Essential to arroz con pollo and many stews.

← Taíno

Venezuela · Colombia

Onoto

The Andean and Caribbean coastal name. Used in Venezuelan hallacas, Colombian tamales, and any dish that wants a warm golden stain without heat.

French Caribbean · France

Roucou / Rocou

The French Caribbean name (roucou) and its metropolitan French form (rocou). Used in Martinique, Guadeloupe, and French Guiana for both the seeds and the traditional reddish paste.

Philippines

Atsuete / Achuete

Introduced via the Manila galleons from Mexico. The seeds are steeped in oil to colour and flavour Filipino stews, rice dishes like kare-kare, and pancit.

← Spanish achiote

Seed vs. condiment

A note on form

Annatto / achiote = the seeds themselves. Recado / achiote paste = the ground-seed paste mixed with garlic, spices, and bitter orange, used as a marinade in Yucatecan and Central American cooking.

Native names

The same seed, its own word

Across Asia the seed shows up in distinct scripts, from local plant names to market and food-label terms. Each spelling is its own thread back to a kitchen.

China

胭脂树

yānzhī shù, “rouge tree”

The seeds (胭脂籽) colour food and appear in traditional medicine; the name borrows the word for women’s rouge.

Japan

ベニノキ

beninoki, “red tree”

The plant’s own Japanese name, written for the deep red its seeds lend to food and dye.

Korea · Hangul

안나토색소

annato saekso, “annatto colourant”

The Korean food-label term for annatto extract, made from the seed coat of Bixa orellana. Culinary references may also write the borrowed names as 아나토 or 아치오테.

India · Hindi & Urdu

लटकन

laṭkan

The common North Indian name for the seed and its colouring paste, sold in spice markets for rich golden-red dishes.

Thailand

คำแสด

kham-saet

A traditional Thai dye for food, cloth, and ceremony. Its own word, long predating the English “annatto.”

Indonesia · Malaysia

kesumba

kesumba (keling)

The Malay-Indonesian name, used across the archipelago as a food and textile colour, distinct from the European trade names.

Vietnam

hạt điều màu

hạt điều màu, “colour seed”

Steeped in hot oil to give bún bò, bánh xèo, and central-Vietnamese stews their warm red glow.
