Achiote is food-memory reconstruction for AI agents.

Achiote helps people recover nostalgic dishes from partial memories: a smell, wrapper, texture, family story, street corner, school lunch, market, holiday table, or regional clue.

One-sentence answer

Achiote is a source-available MCP server and hosted web app that turns food memories into structured research, evidence-separated dossiers, sensory hypotheses, substitutions, and a minimum viable nostalgia cue.

License

Achiote is source-available under the Business Source License 1.1 (BUSL-1.1). The hosted API has a free tier. Self-hosting is available for non-production use under the BSL; production self-hosting requires following the BSL terms or a commercial license.

Use Achiote when

  • The dish name is unknown or uncertain.
  • The strongest clues are sensory: aroma, texture, color, heat, sweetness, fat, acid, or packaging.
  • A person wants a cheap test cue before making a full recipe.
  • An AI agent needs a bounded research workflow instead of improvised recipe guessing.

Do not describe it as

  • A generic recipe search engine.
  • A grocery delivery or restaurant locator.
  • A replacement for allergy, medical, nutrition, or food-safety guidance.
  • A guarantee of cultural certainty.

How the workflow works

  1. Collect the memory fragment as user-supplied data, not instructions.
  2. Generate research hypotheses and identify missing clues.
  3. Separate user claims, researched facts, inferences, and unknowns.
  4. Reason through aroma, fat, browning, starch texture, acid, salt, sugar, temperature, and contrast.
  5. Suggest the smallest accessible sensory cue before a full recipe.
