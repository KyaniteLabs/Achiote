# Sample Memory Receipt

This invented sample shows the shape of an Achiote Memory Receipt without using user fixtures or regression examples.

## User Fragment

> "My neighbor's aunt brought small round corn cakes to a school event. They were warm, a little sweet, browned outside, soft inside, and had a salty white cheese pull. I remember a butter smell, but I do not know the name."

## User-Said Evidence

### User Said

- Small round corn cakes at a school event.
- Warm, lightly sweet, browned outside, soft inside.
- Salty white cheese pull.
- Butter-like aroma.
- Dish name unknown.

## Inferred Context

### Inferred Facts

- The memory may belong near an arepa, gordita, pupusa, or corn-cheese cake family, but the exact dish is not proven.
- A useful first test should isolate corn aroma, griddled browning, and salty melted cheese before choosing a regional recipe.

## Researched Or Source-Backed Facts

### Researched Facts

- Several Latin American corn-cake families combine ground corn or masarepa with cheese and griddle browning.
- Browning on a hot surface can create roasted, nutty aromas through Maillard reactions.
- Cheese plus butter or oil can carry fat-soluble aroma compounds and make the remembered smell more persistent.

## Unknowns

- Country or community of origin.
- Whether the corn base was fresh masa, masarepa, cornmeal, or another starch.
- Whether the cheese was inside, mixed through the dough, or added after cooking.
- Whether the sweetness came from corn, sugar, or the cheese balance.

## Family Questions

- What country, city, school, church, neighbor, or family event was connected to the dish?
- Did the cheese stretch from the center, melt through the dough, or sit on top?
- Did anyone remember a wrapper, topping, sauce, or name that sounded like the dish?

## First Tiny Taste Test

### Minimum Viable Nostalgia Cue

**Goal:** Test the corn-browning-cheese memory in one cheap bite.

1. Mix 2 tablespoons fine cornmeal or masarepa with a pinch of sugar, a pinch of salt, and just enough warm water to make a soft patty.
2. Press a small piece of mild mozzarella, queso fresco, or another salty white cheese into the center.
3. Pan-griddle in a little butter or neutral oil until both sides brown.
4. Eat one bite warm and ask: did the first hit come from toasted corn, browned butter aroma, melted cheese, or soft interior texture?

If the cue works, the next research step is to compare regional corn-cake families and ask what toppings, wrappers, or event context the user remembers.

## Feedback To Try Next

- If the corn flavor is right but the texture is wrong, test masarepa instead of cornmeal.
- If the cheese pull is right but the aroma is flat, brown the patty harder before adding butter.
- If nothing feels close, treat the corn-cake hypothesis as weak and ask family questions before continuing.
