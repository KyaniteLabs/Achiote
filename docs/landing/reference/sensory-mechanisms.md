# Sensory Mechanisms

When exact identity is uncertain, reason from mechanisms. A small cue can test a memory before a recipe exists.

## Aroma

Questions:

- What smell arrives first?
- Is it raw, toasted, fried, fermented, floral, smoky, herbal, dairy, citrus, or spice-bloomed?
- Is the aroma carried by fat, steam, alcohol, acid, or warmth?

Mechanisms:

- fat-soluble aromatics from spices, garlic, onion, chile, citrus peel, herbs;
- volatile aroma release from heat;
- enclosed steam from wrappers, lids, leaves, or cups;
- smoke, char, or toasted notes.

## Browning and Maillard

Questions:

- Was there a browned crust, fried edge, roasted meat smell, toasted grain, or caramel note?
- Was the memory more about surface browning or soft interior?

Mechanisms:

- Maillard reactions from proteins and sugars;
- caramelization;
- toasted starch or grain;
- fried oil aroma.

## Starch texture

Questions:

- Chewy, sticky, fluffy, dense, crumbly, gelatinous, crisp, creamy, or grainy?
- Was it hot, cooled, reheated, steamed, fried, griddled, or soaked?

Mechanisms:

- gelatinized starch;
- retrograded starch;
- masa or root starch body;
- rice, wheat, corn, cassava, potato, taro, plantain, or legume structure.

## Acid, sugar, and salt balance

Questions:

- Did it lean sour, sweet, salty, savory, bitter, or spicy?
- Was the sourness citrus, vinegar, tomato, dairy, fermentation, pickle, tamarind, or fruit?

Mechanisms:

- acid brightens fat and starch;
- sugar rounds bitterness and spice;
- salt increases aroma perception;
- fermentation adds acid plus savory depth.

## Fat and mouth-coating

Questions:

- Did it coat the mouth?
- Was the fat dairy, coconut, meat, seed/nut, oil, or emulsified sauce?

Mechanisms:

- fat carries aromatics;
- emulsions create body;
- dairy/coconut/nut fats change mouthfeel;
- rendered meat fat carries memory through smell.

## Temperature

Questions:

- Was it hot, warm, room temperature, chilled, iced, frozen, or reheated?
- Did the temperature change the smell?

Mechanisms:

- heat releases aroma;
- cold suppresses sweetness and aroma but sharpens texture;
- warm liquids carry memory through steam.

## Texture contrast

Questions:

- Was there crunch plus soft filling?
- Thin liquid plus chewy pieces?
- Crisp edge plus creamy center?

Mechanisms:

- contrast is often the memory trigger;
- one texture alone may miss the cue;
- build the smallest contrast first.

## Proxy by remembered ingredient family

Let the strongest remembered ingredient family choose the cheap proxy. Test the mechanism with ordinary groceries before buying the suspected specialty item.

- peanut memory: test peanut plus sugar or caramel, not a generic nut;
- grainy-sugar or coconut sweet: test sugar crystallization plus toasted coconut or seed aroma before buying the suspected sweet;
- yuca or cassava memory: test cassava-family chew first, potato only as a fallback for body;
- fish memory: test a small fish bite unless a stated restriction rules it out;
- hot-orange-sauce memory: test acid plus chile heat plus color and aroma, not a generic salsa;
- masa or wrapped-starch memory: test a green-starch body plus an enclosed-steam wrapper before sourcing exact leaves.

Safety still overrides this: if the remembered family is or could contain a stated allergen, do not propose it. See the safety boundary in `rules.md`.

## Cue design rule

A minimum viable cue should test one or two mechanisms at a time. If it tests five things at once, it is too close to a recipe and too hard to learn from.
