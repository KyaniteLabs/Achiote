# Source Quality Ladder

Achiote weighs sources by what they can actually prove.

## Core principle

The best source depends on the claim.

A family member is high authority for "what our family remembers," but not for broad culinary history. A grocery page is high authority for "this item appears in this store listing today," but not for regional authenticity. A food-science paper can explain a mechanism, but not identify someone's childhood dish.

## Ladder by claim type

### Dish or drink identity

Strong:

- regional cookbooks or culinary references;
- community sources with language/cultural context;
- bilingual sources matching the user's words;
- multiple independent sources that agree on name, region, and method.

Medium:

- reputable food publications;
- restaurant/menu references with context;
- cooking videos by people from the relevant community.

Weak:

- generic recipe aggregators;
- SEO blogs with no regional context;
- unsourced AI-generated pages.

### Technique and texture

Strong:

- detailed recipes with method explanation;
- cooking videos showing texture/process;
- cookbooks;
- food-science references.

Medium:

- high-quality recipe sites;
- community forum posts with specific technique notes.

Weak:

- short ingredient lists with no method;
- summaries that do not show texture.

### Sensory mechanism

Strong:

- food-science references;
- ingredient chemistry;
- direct cooking process evidence;
- consistent reports across recipes and videos.

Medium:

- expert culinary writing;
- repeated community descriptions.

Weak:

- aesthetic descriptions with no process detail.

### Local availability

Strong:

- current store pages;
- current market pages;
- direct user confirmation;
- delivery or inventory pages with timestamp.

Medium:

- store category pages;
- local market directories;
- common regional store/corridor knowledge.

Weak:

- old blog posts;
- general claims that an ingredient is "easy to find";
- invented prices.

### Professional R&D

Strong:

- peer-reviewed food-science research;
- regulatory or safety references where relevant;
- supplier technical documents;
- sensory evaluation literature;
- controlled prototype notes.

Medium:

- chef/operator case studies;
- trade publications.

Weak:

- trend articles and marketing copy.

## How to report sources

Use source roles:

```text
Source audit
- Identity source:
- Technique source:
- Sensory mechanism source:
- Local availability source:
- Weak or conflicting source:
```

If you cannot verify live sources, say so. Do not pretend.
