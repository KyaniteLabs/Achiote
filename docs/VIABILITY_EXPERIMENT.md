# 30-Day Viability Experiment

Run this before broad paid acquisition. The goal is not vanity traffic; the goal is proof that strangers understand the product, finish a memory, and value Achiote beyond generic AI.

## Target

- Recruit 30 strangers who have a real half-remembered family, childhood, regional, restaurant, or travel food memory.
- Each session starts with a messy fragment, not a known recipe name.
- Each person should reach a Memory Receipt and first tiny taste test in under 3 minutes.

## Pass Gates

- At least 40% say it feels more useful than generic AI for this job.
- At least 10% pay, preorder, buy a Memory Pack, start Personal, or commit to a paid Family Archive Sprint.
- At least 60% produce a useful next question for family or a tiny taste test they would actually try.
- At least 70% understand that Achiote separates user-said evidence, inferred context, researched facts, and unknowns.
- The ratio `feedback_closer / ask_succeeded` should improve week over week, or written feedback must identify a fixable reason it is not improving.

## Kill Or Pause Gates

- If fewer than 10% pay or credibly commit after 30 strangers, do not scale paid acquisition.
- If most users compare it to generic recipe search, pause marketing and tighten the opening screen.
- If users do not understand the Memory Receipt, simplify the receipt before adding more features.
- If the first tiny taste test feels too hard, reduce effort before improving research depth.

## Weekly Review

- Week 1: validate the first screen and whether strangers know what to type.
- Week 2: validate Memory Receipt comprehension and family-question usefulness.
- Week 3: validate willingness to pay for Personal, Memory Pack, and Family Archive Sprint.
- Week 4: decide whether to scale, reposition, niche down, or stop.

## Required Evidence

- Count of strangers tested.
- Count who finished under 3 minutes.
- Count who said it feels more useful than generic AI.
- Count who paid or made a credible paid commitment.
- Top five reasons the answer felt close.
- Top five reasons the answer felt wrong, too generic, too hard, or culturally overconfident.
